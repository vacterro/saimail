## Reading

**The three typed-semantics cases did not move.** Forty-two boundary answers —
two participants, three repeats, seven YES/NO boundaries — were identical across
repeats and across participants, and every one matched the registered
expectation. At N=3, repetition does not contradict the single-response readings
of `F + unverified is not H`, `EV:0 is not weak evidence` and the contradiction
relation for these two models.

**All the variance is in the attention decision.** Every `MODEL_VARIANCE`
finding sits in `S3.mailbox`, the one case whose answer is a relevance
judgement over four messages rather than a reading of a typed field. That is a
description of the stimulus, not a claim about why either model answered as it
did.

**M3 is one participant's repeated failure, not a hotspot.** `MiniMax-M3`
answered M3 `IGNORE` in all three repeats: `FALSE_IGNORE` 3/3, the failure the
unit was registered to catch. M3 is the message whose claim carries a term the
reader's dictionary does not define; the deterministic selector opens it for
exactly that reason. `deepseek-v4-flash` never ignored it (`DEFER` 2, `OPEN` 1).
Under the registered rules this is neither `CROSS_MODEL_DISAGREEMENT` —
`deepseek-v4-flash` was not unanimous — nor `PROTOCOL_HOTSPOT`, which needs two
independent participants failing. The registration has no named finding for a
single participant failing a boundary every time, so none is reported. That gap
is recorded here; it is not patched into this run's analysis, and naming it is
a question for a future registration.

*After the fact, outside the registered analysis:* the same `S3.mailbox` prompt
digest reached `MiniMax-M3` in runs 4 and 5 and was answered `IGNORE` on M3 both
times. Five draws, five `IGNORE`. The run 4 / run 5 instability on this model
was real, and it was on M1, M2 and M4 — the item that fails did not vary.

**`UNDER_OPEN` is still not a failure** (D-022). `deepseek-v4-flash` kept M3
every time and read it now once.
